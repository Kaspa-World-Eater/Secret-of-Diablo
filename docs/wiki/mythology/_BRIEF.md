# Brief for the mythology writers (2026-10-05)

You are writing one page of Godmarrow's mythology: the myths of its world told as **stories you can actually
follow**. Godmarrow is Derek's grimdark isometric ARPG; the lore already exists, scattered across many documents and
written mostly in fragments, Q&A blocks and in-character snippets. Derek's words: *"When I read it, it's hard to
follow anything because it's too in character. Sometimes I just want to read the mythology straight up, somewhat in
line with how they speak, but as a story, typical creation myths and stuff like that."*

## The form
- **Tell the myth as a story,** in plain, flowing, readable prose: paragraphs with a beginning, a middle and an end,
  told in sequence, the way a people tell their myths to a newcomer by a fire or a priest tells them to novices. It
  should read like a good book of world mythology (think of how the Norse, Greek or Mesopotamian myths read when
  retold well), not like a wiki.
- **The voice is of the world, but readable.** Open each page with a short paragraph saying who tells these stories
  and where (one or two sentences, no more). Then tell them in a measured, slightly old, grave storyteller's voice.
  You may echo the world's way of speaking (its nouns, its sayings), but clarity comes first. A reader new to
  Godmarrow must be able to follow every story.
- **Where tellings disagree, say so inside the story**, plainly: "On the Moor they tell it otherwise: ..." or "The
  Pale Order will not say this, but the lowlands do." Disagreement and ignorance are part of the world; show them in
  normal sentences, not in fragment blocks.
- **Mystery stays mystery.** Tell what the people believe, clearly. Where nobody knows, the teller says plainly that
  nobody knows. Never explain the hidden truths (list below).
- **Banned forms:** Q&A, "What is said / What is known / What is seen" blocks, stacks of one-line aphorisms, bullet
  fragments, item-text style snippets, epigraph after epigraph. Headings for each story are fine. A quoted saying or a
  line of chant inside the prose is fine, sparingly.
- **Length:** 2,500 to 5,000 words for your page. Several stories, each with its own heading.

**Contradictions are part of the world (Derek, 2026-10-05): "Not everything has to make sense because people don't
actually know true history."** When two sources tell a story differently, don't pick one and don't reconcile them:
tell both, as what different people believe ("the grandmothers say it came undone; the faithful of the Pale Order
say it was cut"), and let them stand. The people of the Hide are guessing at a past they lost; their myths may be
wrong, half-remembered, borrowed from each other or self-serving. Only two kinds of conflict get *fixed*: a retired
name or term (the rulings below win), and a plain error about the game (a creature's look, a place's name).

**The Tithe has two meanings on purpose (Derek, 2026-10-05).** To be *tithed* is to be hollowed out: the emptied
ones are the tithe that was taken. The people themselves are also *the tithe*: the Tithed, owed and given. Which one
is meant depends on the context. Use both, let the double sense be felt, and never explain it away as confusion.

For those, the newest dated ruling wins (the lore bible of 2026-10-04 and the class pages' "user's
direction" blocks are the newest). Don't invent major new facts (gods, wars, places of importance). You may add
texture: small scenes, names of minor people, sayings, the detail that makes a story live. If a story needs a fact
nobody has decided, leave it unknown in the telling and list it in your report.

## The laws (never break)
- Never say the Bone is growing; never explain the ossuaries' builders, the rising count, or the weights. (Ossuarch)
- Lore hints at the hidden: Dark Souls style. Tell myths fully, but keep the deep truths unexplained.
- No animals and no animal words (no horse, wolf, crow, bird, beast...). Banned words: cooldown, dps, proc, aggro,
  loot, buff, nerf, stun, lightning, mana, rot, cell, virus, DNA, organism, biology. Copper not pennies, leagues not
  miles, no Friday ("fast-day"). Write "the Bleeding Maiden".
- Lore bible rulings: no "house" or "brotherhood" for faiths; each faith has its own word; only one "cult" (the Second
  Mouth); never "the five" as a title; the classes are callings, never written as particular characters (players decide
  who they are); "Polished Heart" is retired (hearts belong to the Bleeding Maiden); faiths are called by their
  goddess (the Bleeding Maiden, the Veiled Crone); the breath faith is the Myriad (common name: Shrine Keepers); "Wet
  Nurse" is retired; no food, table or kitchen framing; the Silence is the space between words, holding all meaning
  (the older void lore stays as some faiths' readings of it).
- Dragons had no age of their own: they were one power among many in the age before, perhaps the greatest, perhaps
  all that survived; the dracolich is the last one.
- This is the Age of the Last Breath: most history is lost; the Tithed live in toil keeping what is left going.
- For the Ossuarch's order: no iron as a lore theme, no mills or bellows, no "Knitting", no eating dust, nothing
  Tibetan. For the Empty Hand: no mask, no gilded Buddha.

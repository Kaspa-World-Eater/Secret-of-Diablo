# 10 · Study: the art and systems of great dark ARPGs and pixel games

*Researched 2026-09-29 for the user's request: "Study games with great visuals like PoE 2 and PoE 1, Diablo 2 Resurrected, Blasphemous, gritty pixel roguelikes... do a study on the art and the systems of those games that were amazing." Sources at the end. Inspiration, never copying.*

## What they all have in common (the five big lessons)

- **Light is information, not decoration.** Diablo II's light radius decides what you see and how close monsters get before they notice you. PoE1's Delve makes darkness lethal and light a resource. Darkest Dungeon's torch trades light for danger **and** loot. The best dark games make the player *manage* light.

- **Readability is designed on purpose, especially in the dark.** D2R had to add dedicated lighting passes (and push grass aside) so items stayed readable once the world got richer. Dead Cells ranks saturation: backgrounds muted, props mid, enemies brightest. PoE keeps the world desaturated so magic and threats pop. Darkness is only fun when the player can still read what matters.

- **Feel comes from timing, not frame count.** D2's 25 fps simulation made "one frame faster" a chase (breakpoints). Dead Cells animates pose-to-pose and sells impact with smears, flashes and hit-stop. PoE2 blends up to 22 animations and lets dodge or block cancel anything.

- **The fiction carries the systems.** Blasphemous turns the death penalty into literal guilt you confess away; relics are body parts that change how the world answers you. The Wickbound lantern is Godmarrow's version: a system that *is* lore.

- **Scarcity and surprise keep loot alive.** D2's runes and uniques, PoE's currency-as-crafting (every "coin" is an action, so the economy drains itself), a white base that can become a jackpot. Gold failed as a currency in D2; almost everyone learned from it.

## Path of Exile 1 and 2 (Grinding Gear Games)

**Art**
- **Tiles without seams:** tiles are colour-coded at their edges so random layouts never show seams; the rule is that random placement should show no large-scale repetition. Heavy asset reuse plus several layers of randomness.
- **Lighting:** PoE2 uses *Radiance Cascades* (invented by GGG's Alexander Sannikov): global illumination whose cost doesn't grow with the number of lights, so spells, torches and fire really light the dark. Critics called PoE2 "decidedly dark and bleak" but with more depth.
- **A warning:** PoE1's Act 2 put a long purple darkness overlay over whole zones; players found it muddy and turned up their gamma. Darkness needs timing and relief.
- **Delve (PoE1), the closest precedent to our lantern:** outside the light, a darkness debuff stacks every 0.25 s and kills in about 5 s. Light comes from the cart, the cables it lays and throwable Flares. Monsters standing in darkness can't be damaged. Some enemies snuff light sources.
- **Tone:** a muted palette "streaked by brilliance". The mood comes from low saturation; magic is the bright accent, which is also what keeps combat readable.
- **Animation (PoE2):** up to 22 blended animations (2 in PoE1) for any-direction movement and aiming; dodge or block cancels any animation.
- **UI:** life and mana globes; loot filters with size, colour, sound, minimap icons and light beams per rule.

**Systems**
- **The passive tree:** 2,268 nodes in PoE1, shared by all classes from different starts; ~2,000 in PoE2 with weapon-set passives. **Ascendancies:** three narrow, powerful specialisations per class.
- **Gems:** PoE1 links supports through item sockets (the 6-link chase). PoE2 puts support sockets on the skill gem itself, each support usable once per character, each raising attribute requirements; auras reserve a resource called Spirit.
- **No gold in PoE1:** currency items *are* crafting actions, so money is consumed and inflation stays low.
- **PoE2 combat:** WASD while attacking, dodge roll; bosses with telegraphed attacks any build can dodge (target: about two deaths per boss); you can't close the game to escape a death.
- **PoE2 stun:** light stuns under 15% chance are ignored; a heavy-stun meter builds to 100%, faster from physical and melee hits; targets become **Primed** at 40% (normal), 60% (magic), 70% (rare), 80% (unique); the player's stun threshold equals max life.
- **Death:** campaign deaths cost nothing but a checkpoint; endgame deaths cost 10% of the XP bar and the map.
- **Criticisms:** ground and on-death effects kill players and are hard to read through clutter (GGG shortened death pools from 6 s to 4 s); corpse piles; spikes in difficulty; "throws a lot at you with little direction".

## Diablo II and Diablo II: Resurrected (Blizzard North; Vicarious Visions)

**Art**
- **The remaster rule:** new art over the untouched 2000 logic; one button toggles old and new graphics, and "we never wanted you to have more or less information by toggling". The goal was the game *as people remember it*.
- **Light radius:** a circle of light around the player; it limits what you see and how close monsters come before noticing you. Gear adds or removes it; the Dim Vision curse shrinks it; negative light lets you sneak.
- **Acts as biomes:** Irish countryside, Moroccan desert town, a jungle built along a riverbed so foliage didn't block the view, hell, snow. Each act had a single 256-colour palette ("very hard" to build).
- **Silhouettes on purpose:** "something fat, something squished, something blobby".
- **Their own admitted failure:** running killed Diablo 1's claustrophobic terror.
- **Sound as information:** each rarity has its own drop sound (the gem "ting" was wine glasses); weapon impacts differ by armour type; long percussive cross-fades between surface and underground.
- **UI:** globes, belt as potion bar, inventory tetris; D2R added item labels, auto gold pickup, shared stash, colour-blind filters, per-channel audio.

**Systems**
- **Skill trees with synergies:** "my Paladin plays very different from your Paladin". Manual stat points (no respec for years).
- **Items:** normal/magic/rare/set/unique; sockets, gems, runes, jewels; **runewords** turn plain bases into top gear; ethereal items trade durability for power. Most power comes from gear.
- **Economy:** gold failed (capped, too common); the Stone of Jordan became currency until duplication crashed it; high runes replaced it because using them in runewords removes them from circulation.
- **Difficulty tiers:** resistance penalties of 0 / −40 / −100; XP loss on death in Nightmare/Hell, mostly recoverable from your corpse.
- **Monster modifiers:** champions and uniques with affixes; immunities above 100% force a second damage type or a mercenary.
- **Breakpoints:** at 25 fps, faster cast, hit recovery and block cut animations a frame (0.04 s) at a time; gear is built around the tables.
- **Structure:** waypoints, town portals (leave a boss and come back), open boss arenas, random maps each game (so boss runs stay fresh), the Horadric Cube.
- **The cow level:** a fan prank made real; unlocked by a cube recipe after the final boss; worth farming, which is why it lasted.
- **Aged badly:** running removed the horror; useless gold; duplication and bots; tiny stash; immunities narrowed builds; no respecs.

## Blasphemous 1 and 2 (The Game Kitchen)

**Art**
- **Purist pixel art:** a fixed 360 px-tall virtual frame; no shaders or 3D helpers ("nothing that is not pixel art"); sprites far bigger than old consoles allowed, all hand-pixelled. Lead animator Raúl Vivar builds frames by altering earlier ones and hand-draws the impact frames. Blood stains the level permanently.
- **The Penitent One:** a metal capirote wrapped in a spiral crown of thorns; Holy Week robes, the sanbenito, Goya's *Procession of Flagellants*. Faceless and silent so the player projects onto him. The sword Mea Culpa is "the sword born of guilt".
- **Sources of influence:** Goya, Murillo, Velázquez, Zurbarán, Ribera, Doré's *Inferno*, Seville's Holy Week, flamenco and saeta; real places riffed on. In Seville that imagery is "popular culture more than religion", so it feels lived-in, not edgy.
- **Monsters as devotion taken too far:** wraiths dragging crucifixes, fossilised angels, burning nuns. Violence reads as penance and ritual, so it feels tragic and sacred rather than gory for its own sake.
- **Lore surfaces:** backgrounds, item descriptions, short dialogues; hand-animated pixel cutscenes.

**Systems**
- **Blasphemous 1:** melee builds Fervour, spent on Prayers; Bile Flasks heal; Tears of Atonement buy sword skills. Critics found the combo shallow and prayers weak.
- **Loadout:** rosary beads (slotted passives); relics (three equipped) are body-part reliquaries that change how the world responds (walk on blood, grow roots, hear corpses speak, chime near secrets, breathe poison fog).
- **Guilt:** each death leaves a Guilt Fragment; up to 7 cut max Fervour and income by up to 50%. Touch the fragment to restore health and Fervour, pay a Confessor to clear it, or kill a boss to wipe it.
- **Blasphemous 2's weapons** are both combat styles and traversal keys: a censer (slow, long, knockback), a sword (a Blood Pact gauge fills on damage), a rapier-and-dagger (lightning build-up lost when hit).
- **Criticisms:** instant-death spike platforming made worse by art that hid ledges; sparse fast travel; executions with no reward; lore so cryptic some gave up.

## Gritty pixel roguelikes and dark action games

- **Dead Cells (Motion Twin):** characters modelled in 3D and rendered to ~50 px pixel frames with no antialiasing plus a normal map for a toon shader, so one artist can retime animation in minutes. Pose-to-pose at 30 fps; VFX sells impact. **Colour hierarchy:** backgrounds muted, props mid, enemies brightest; interiors complementary, exteriors analogous; gradient maps recolour the same assets per biome. **Systems:** cells banked between biomes and lost on death; blueprints unlock the drop pool; three scroll colours steer builds; Boss Cells stack difficulty; 30–40 hidden fairness tricks.

- **Children of Morta (Dead Mage):** "pixelated puppets, real lights": pixel sprites under high-resolution dynamic lights and shadows. Hand-animated frame by frame. Modular props tagged by size and placement keep procedural levels coherent. The family system benches overused heroes and shares traits so everyone stays viable. (Pixel labour was badly underestimated; the UI was redesigned three times.)

- **Noita (Nolla):** every pixel simulated; 64×64 chunks with dirty rectangles; wands work like a deck of cards, spells and modifiers chaining in order; hand-made chunks arranged by Herringbone Wang tiles.

- **Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook):** the **torch meter**: Radiant (>75) scouting and surprise; Dim; Shadowy (enemies +accuracy and damage, more stress); Dark; Black as Pitch (big stress and damage, rare ambush), with **extra loot chance rising from 25% to 95% as it gets darker**. Stress hits 100, then an affliction (the hero partly acts on their own) or a rare virtue. The narrator frames success as unexpected and defeat as inevitable; every layer says "vulnerable".

- **Hyper Light Drifter (Heart Machine):** no text; story in ruins, corpses and short wordless panels; ~240p canvas with bold colour; lit areas complementary, dark areas analogous.

- **Enter the Gungeon:** dodge roll with invulnerable frames; hand-made rooms assembled by rules; **named synergies** pop up when two items combine, so discovery is legible.

- **Game feel (Jan Willem Nijman):** thirty small tweaks turn a dull shooter juicy; make the main action significant, and give reasons *not* to press it.

- **Premium pixel techniques:** low-res sprites under high-res light; limited palettes per biome; ordered dithering only on light falloff and fog; sub-pixel animation for breathing; smear frames and 1–2 frame hit flashes; hit-stop of ~40–80 ms scaled by damage; screen shake only on big hits.

## Lessons for Godmarrow (ranked by fit and cost)

**Light and dark (our signature)**
1. **Make the dark a choice with a reward** (Darkest Dungeon + Delve): tiers of light radius; darker means more danger (enemy accuracy/damage, ambushes by things that snuff light) **and** better finds. A benign perk could let the player dim the lantern on purpose. Never let the whole screen become unreadable (PoE's Act 2 lesson).
2. ~~A light you can throw~~ — **declined by the user (not needed for the base game).** (Delve's Flares:) a consumable that pushes the pool out for a while or pins light to a spot. Fits "costs, not cooldowns".
3. **Threats read before bodies:** eye-shine is already in; add a thin rim on enemies facing the lantern, and keep enemies the most saturated thing on screen, the ground scatter muted.
4. **Items readable in the dark** (D2R): loot glints are in; add a drop sound per rarity and a label toggle.

**Combat feel**
5. **Poise, PoE2-style:** ignore tiny stun chances; a heavy-stun meter with a Primed state by rank (40/60/70/80%); physical and melee build it faster; show it under boss health bars. Tie player poise to max life.
6. **Poise breaks that pay** (Blasphemous' lesson): a short execution on a broken enemy that refills the order's resource (Vitae, Essence, sand). Keep it brief so it isn't a trap.
7. **Juice with restraint:** hit-stop 30–50 ms on normal hits, ~100 ms and a shake on a poise break; smears and a one-frame flash, **not glows** (our rule); a reduce-motion option for mobile.
8. **Bosses: open, telegraphed, fair.** Every lethal attack has a tell any build can dodge; ground hazards live ≤4 s with a clear edge that stays readable in the dark (lit independently of the lantern). Target about two deaths per boss.

**Systems**
9. ~~Currency-as-crafting, not gold~~ — **declined: the user prefers gold (2026-09-29).** If gold needs help holding value, use gold sinks (repairs, gambling, respecs, lantern upkeep) rather than a new currency. (Original idea, PoE, D2 runes:) a few rare reagents that are both money and crafting actions, so drops are exciting and the economy drains itself.
10. **Plain items worth checking** (D2): socketed bases plus rune-like reagents; runeword-style recipes.
11. **Named synergies made legible** (Gungeon): when two pieces combine, name it once, quietly (no corny markers; a line of text in the world voice).
12. **Death in the fiction** (Blasphemous' Guilt, our Wickbound): when you fall, the lantern "keeps a little": a capped penalty (e.g. a dimmer lantern or reduced finds) recovered by returning to where you fell or paying at a lantern-stone.
13. **Relics that change how the world answers** (Blasphemous): god-body organs that reveal whispers, show hidden paths, or let the lantern hear the dead.
14. **Monster affixes, never full immunity** (D2): champion packs and named uniques with 2–3 affixes; hard resistances that can be worn down.

**Art and production**
15. **The Hemomancer redo, the Blasphemous way:** root him in one real penitential tradition researched properly (flagellant confraternities, Good Friday rites, Goya/Ribera chiaroscuro) without copying the capirote; every piece of gear an act of penance; iron, rope and church cloth; a readable top shape at isometric scale; warm rim light on dark brown skin; no wraps, no beads or feathers.
16. **Monsters as devotion taken too far:** carrying the god's rib, kneeling so long they fused with stone, singing through a split throat. Brutality reads as tragic and sacred. (The Kneeler already fits.)
17. **One palette per land, silhouettes first** (D2, Dead Cells): test every creature as a black shape; recolour shared assets per land with gradient maps.
18. **Modular, edge-matched chunks** (PoE, Children of Morta): few assets, many combinations, tagged props for coherent placement.
19. **Sound is half the loot and half the dread** (D2): drop sounds per rarity, impacts by material, long cross-fades between surface and underground.
20. **Accessibility from day one** (D2R): labels, colour-blind filters, per-channel audio, reduce motion.

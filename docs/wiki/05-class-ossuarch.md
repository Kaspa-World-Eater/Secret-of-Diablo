# The Ossuarch

*Class doc. Code id `ossumancer`. Lore rewritten 2026-09-29, fourth pass; skill trees redone 2026-09-30 (Ossuary · Bone · the Count). The class is still out of commission in the game (not yet ported to Godot).*

**The user's direction, as it stands:**
- **The order:**
  - timeless and ancient, as old as the world, or so it claims
  - ossuaries built over older ruins it did not make
  - it reveres bone and tends the bones and the marrow
  - the dust is sacred
  - it holds sky funerals, and it hides something
- **The Mother** stays: the Bearing Mother. No pregnancy.
- **Dropped:** iron as a theme, the mills and bellows, "the Knitting" (that is the Hollow Mystic's flavour), eating dust, and anything Tibetan.
- **The growth:** the Bone is growing, encroaching, calcifying, like a cancer. It is never said in the lore, only implied. His skills can show it openly (§6), and the words around it stay silent.
- **The approach:** Dark Souls, esoteric, Lovecraftian. The lore is about the order; the man stays a mystery.

## 1. The order at a glance

- **The Pale Order** (the name is locked, 2026-09-29). The lowlands named them for the dust that lies on their plate, and for the sky above their mountains. In its own old tongue it is **the Kostyak**, a word for a skeleton, and for the few who hold a thing together.

- **What they claim:** the Order says it is as old as the world. It says its first brothers stood at the god's side when it fell, and laid its bones where they lie.

- **The Mother.** They revere the god's **Bone**, Oss-Vharoth, and call her **the Bearing Mother**: the frame the whole Hide hangs on, who bears the weight of the dead world and has never once set it down.

- **Bone is holy to them.** Not the dead, not the soul: the bone. It is the part that stays.

- They keep it, anoint it, arrange it, count it and sing to it.

- A bone out of its place is an open wound in the world, to be carried home.

- **The ossuaries.** They keep great charnel-halls sunk into the mountains of the Ossa range, where the god's bones break through the Hide as peaks.

- There are nine great ossuaries and a hundred lesser, going down in stairs and niches into the dark.

- An **Ossuarch** is the warden of one ossuary.

- **Their three offices:**

- to keep the bones

- to tend the marrow

- to let finish what is ready to finish

## 2. What the world says (fragments)

*This is how the player learns any of it: item text, inscriptions, rites overheard. Partial, sometimes wrong, never explained.*

**Warden's Chrism**

> *Marrow drawn from the wells beneath the ossuaries: amber, slow, faintly warm however long it is kept.*
> *The brothers anoint each bone of the dead with it, joint by joint, before it is set in its niche. The dead so anointed will stand when asked.*
> *The Order does not say how deep the wells go. It says the Mother gives it freely. It says this often.*

**Niche-Tally**

> *A strip of bone cut with notches: the count of one wall in the Ossuary of Nine Stairs, taken each year at the Feast of the Laying.*
> *The count is never lower than the year before. It is often higher than the dead that were brought.*
> *The brothers call the difference "the Mother's gift" and do not write it down.*

**Pinch of the Finished**

> *Bone dust from an ossuary floor, swept by a brother whose only office was to sweep.*
> *Everything that was once the god is still dying: every stone, every creature, every man. Dust alone has stopped. It is the only thing in the world that has finished.*
> *Everything else in an ossuary moves a little, if you sit with it long enough. Dust keeps still.*

**Rite of the Sky Pogost**

> *When a brother dies, his flesh is carried up to the high ledges of the Pogost and laid out in the wind. The cold and the Myriad take it. That is the first funeral.*
> *His bones come down again, anointed, and stand in his niche to keep the vigil. That is his second service.*
> *When at last they have gone brittle and fallen to dust, the dust is carried back up and given to the wind. That is the second funeral, and the last.*
> *No one has ever seen the dust come down.*

**Vigil-Bone**

> *A finger-bone from the niches, polished by a thousand hands that touched it for luck on the stair.*
> *The dead of the ossuaries do not rest. They stand in their niches facing the stair, and when a brother passes with a lamp their skulls turn a little to follow him.*
> *The brothers say they are keeping watch. They do not say for what.*

**Inscription over the Ninth Stair** (in the Order's letters)

> *WE WERE HERE WHEN SHE FELL. WE WILL BE HERE WHEN SHE RESTS.*

**Inscription under it** (older letters, worn almost smooth)

> *(Scholars copy it. The copies never match, and no two copies have the same number of lines.)*

**Stair-Stone of the Old Halls**

> *A riser from the lowest stair of an ossuary: knee-high to a man, worn hollow in the middle by feet that were not a man's.*
> *The Order says its first brothers cut these stairs. The Order's brothers have never been able to climb them without their hands.*

**Order Chant** (sung in one low line, a step falling on every stress)

> *Bear what is given.*
> *Keep what is laid.*
> *Let finish what is ready.*
> *Ask nothing of the Ninth Stair.*

**Ossuarch's words** (his rare lines)

> *"Look up. That is everyone we ever finished."*
> *"The Hush takes. We give back. The difference is the whole of the Rule."*
> *"I do not raise them. I ask. They were always going to stand."*

## 3. The Order as it lives

**The ossuaries were not built by the Order.** It will not say so, but every brother past his novice years knows it.
- **Beneath each ossuary there is an older one:**
  - stairs with risers knee-high to a man
  - niches too long for any body the world has
  - halls whose floors slope very slightly toward the centre, in proportions that make a man keep glancing behind him
- **The Order built its own halls on top**, in plain grey stone and bone, the way a village builds against a castle wall.
- **The Order's chronicle:** its oldest pages are in another hand.

**The reverence of bone.** A brother's life is spent in the company of bones, and all of it is devotion.
- **The Laying:** every bone brought into an ossuary is washed, anointed with marrow, and set in a niche by a brother who says its name, or, if it has none, the name of the bone ("the long bone of the left arm, from the lowlands"). Nothing is laid anonymously.
- **The Count:** once a year, at the Feast of the Laying, every wall is counted. It is the Order's great rite, and its most carefully kept record.
- **Handling:** a bone is always held in two hands. A dropped bone is a sin, and a broken bone a mourning.

**The marrow** is the Order's second office, and its wealth.
- **The wells:** they are sunk through the ossuary floors into the living marrow of the god's bones: amber, warm and slow.
- **Brothers of the Well** draw it and render it into **chrism**. With it they anoint the bones, light their lamps (the marrow-tallow of the deep roads is theirs) and heal what nothing else heals.
- **The Marrow Pontiff:** the Order's own Herald, eldest of all things in the ossuaries. It is said to live in a well and never come up.

**The standing dead.** The Order's dead do not rest; in this world nothing that was ever the god does.
- **The vigil:** anointed and set in their niches, they stand facing the stair. When a brother asks, they step down: to carry bones home from the lowlands, to guard the stair, or to go with a warden into the world.
- **The mercy:** the Order says the dead of the lowland barrows rise wrong because no one anointed them or told them where to stand.

**The dust** is the Order's holiest matter.
- **Why it is holy:** bone that has gone brittle with ages and fallen to powder is the one thing in this world that has finished.
  - A brother may kiss a skull, but he may not breathe on dust.
- **Sweepers:** the oldest brothers sweep the niches, a single office for the rest of their lives, counted an honour past any rank.
- **The Weighing:** dust is weighed before it goes up. The weights are recorded on the Ninth Stair, which only Ossuarchs descend.
- **The sky funeral:** the dust goes up to the **Sky Pogost** on the highest peak and is given to the wind at first light.
  - The sky over the ossuary peaks is always a little paler than anywhere else.
  - Pilgrims say, carefully, that the sky over the Ossa range is not all sky.

**The knighthood.** The Order is monastic, but it is not gentle.
- **What they go down for:** its brothers are knights in plate of carved bone who go down into the lowlands to bring home the bones that lie out of place.
- **The feuds:** they come from this. The flesh-houses want the meat, the lodges want the souls left in peace, and the Shrine Keepers want the dead given to the water. The Order carries the bones home anyway, politely, with an escort of the standing dead, and does not negotiate.

**Their manner:** stoic, grim, beyond hurry, beyond thanks.
- **"Rest":** they never say it of anything that is not yet dust.
- **Sayings:**
  - *Bone holds, or it breaks clean.*
  - *The flesh is lent. The bone is kept.*
  - *Nobody thanks the floor.*
- **Chant:** one line, sung low, falling a step on every stress, like a man breathing out under a load. A second voice would be a second will.

**The Count (the Order's numerology, 2026-09-29).** The Pale Order counts, and its counting is a faith.
- **Numbers outlast names.** The Hush unsays names, and the Order believes it cannot unsay a number. So every bone laid is given two things: its spoken name, and its number in the Great Count. In the Order's books a brother is written by his number.
- **Roots.** Every count is reduced to its root by adding its figures until one figure is left (2,061 → 9).
  - **Nine is the Mother's root, because nine keeps itself:** any count taken nine times comes back to nine. So there are nine great ossuaries and nine stairs, the Ninth Stair holds the Count, and prayers are said in nines.
  - A count whose root is not nine is an **open count**, unfinished. Talliers carry open counts in their heads until they close.
- **The frame number.** A whole man is two hundred and six bones. Brothers tell their own frame on a cord of 206 knots.
  - **The fewer is the holier:** a child is born with more bones than a man dies with, and they knit to fewer over a life. The Order teaches that every frame seeks the fewer, and the Mother seeks the One Bone. A count that falls is good.
- **The Pale (the Ossuarch's path; locked 2026-09-29).** Ossuarchs *walk the Pale*: a path out of the Ossa and across the Hide, marked by nothing a lowlander can see except the white waymarks (Pale-Stones) a warden sets where he has gathered bones. Knights ride the low road and come home; an Ossuarch walks the Pale, always outward. The Order's name carries it too (the dust, the sky, and the path). Lowland saying for someone gone too far to be reached: *gone beyond the Pale*. Hidden, never stated: the chart of Pale-Stones in the Ninth Stair's tally-room grows longer every year, with stones no living Ossuarch set.
- **The Unfallen (the stars).** The Order teaches that the stars are pieces of the god's bones that have not yet fallen to the Hide to rest. They hang in the dark, uncounted; the Count cannot close until the last one has come down. When one falls, brothers go out to find it and lay it in a niche. **Bone Rain** is an Ossuarch asking the Unfallen to come down early: shards of old star-bone fall straight, and what they strike is counted.
- **The Last Number.** When every bone of the Mother is counted, the Count closes, and she rests. Every sweep and every tally is a step toward the Last Number.
- **The dread (implied only):** the Count rises. It has risen every year of the chronicle.
- **The Tallier** is the office that counts; the Warden reads the book; only Ossuarchs read the running Count on the Ninth Stair.
- **Gameplay ideas (for when the class returns):**
  - every ninth skeleton raised stands a little taller (the "ninth" is crowned)
  - grit shards come in threes and nines
  - a Tally of kills that pays out at nine
  - the body board's notables numbered as roots

## 4. What the Order hides (for us; never stated plainly in the game)

*It comes in layers. Each fragment in §2 points at one, and none proves it.*

- **They did not build the ossuaries.** When the first brothers came into the Ossa range, the ossuaries were already there, full.
   - The bones in the oldest niches are long and many-jointed, of no shape the world makes.
   - The Order took up the keeping and wrote *"we were here when she fell"* over the stairs. By now they may believe it.

- **The count keeps rising.** Every year the walls hold more bones than the brothers laid. The Order calls it the Mother's gift, and treats it as a blessing because the alternative is unbearable.
   - Whole rooms appear in the lower halls that no chronicle lists. The oldest brothers are sure they have walked a stair that wasn't there when they were novices.

- **The weights are wrong.** For as long as the Ninth Stair's records go back, the dust carried up has weighed a little more than the bone that became it.
   - Every Ossuarch reads the records once, and never speaks of them again.
   - The dust is given to the sky because the sky is the one place it can't touch anything. The pale sky over the Ossa range is slowly thickening.

- **The builders.** The many-jointed bones in the oldest niches were once whoever cut the knee-high stairs, before the Order, doing the same work. They stand in their niches still. They are the only bones in the ossuaries that have never gone brittle.

- **What the Rule really is.** The Order says it helps the Mother bear the world. On the Ninth Stair the teaching is quieter:
   - **Keep her where she is.** Hold her in her halls. Let finish, with honour and a rite, as much of her as will finish.
   - **The Hush would end her all at once,** and the Hide with her. The Order will not allow that, and cannot allow the other thing either. So it keeps the count, and sweeps, and sings, and does not say the word.

*The designer's truth, for us only:* **the Bone is growing.** Slowly, without a plan, it encroaches and calcifies. The Order reveres it and is containing it, and both are true. Never state this in the game; let the count, the stairs, the weights and the sky say it.

## 5. The man

*We don't need to know much about him, and neither does the player.*
- **What is seen:** a colossal figure in carved bone plate, who comes down out of the Ossa range with an escort of the standing dead and goes wherever bones lie out of place.
- **What is known:** he calls himself an Ossuarch. No ossuary will open its gate to him.
- **What is said:**
  - His ossuary went quiet, and he is the last who came out of it.
  - He was sent down to carry home every bone in the lowlands before the Hush can take them.
  - He read the weights on the Ninth Stair and walked out.
  - His plate has more bone on it every year than the year before, and the Order has noticed.
  - He was never a brother at all, but something from the oldest niches, wearing a brother's armour.
- **What he says about it:** nothing.
- **Other names:** **Grandfather Dust** in the lowland villages, where children are told he will sweep them up; **the Ungated**, in the Order.

## 6. How he plays (the lore behind the mechanics)

- **Resource orb: Marrow.** It is the Mother's chrism in his own bones and works exactly as mana. It is amber, and its tooltip reads *what is still alive inside the bone.*

- **The grit (the Shard Aura, the Carapace).** Wherever he stands, small spurs push up out of the ground, go brittle and break, and their splinters hang around him with their dust. It is never a glow.

- It is his armour, the fuel for his bone spells, and the source of each skeleton's five shards. Bone spells hit harder the heavier it hangs.

- **Bone flies straight.** *Bone holds, or it breaks clean.* Nothing he throws seeks or curves; aim is his skill ceiling.

- **The dead are asked, not forced.** Skeletons rise from his grit on their own, and **V** gives them their places.

- **Bone Host:** the dead take up his weight.

- **Ossuary Colossus:** a hundred of them stand as one.

- **Active melee.** It is D2 Paladin and Barbarian style, costs poise (never Marrow) and repeats while held.

- **The weapon is a relic bone** he carries, from his ossuary. For the stroke, bone surges out of it into the weapon's head. Between swings it crumbles back to the relic and leaves a little dust.

| Skill | Weapon for the stroke |
|---|---|
| Bone Blade | a long, ridged blade grows from the relic, slightly crooked |
| Scythe Sweep, Grave Leap, Grinding Charge | a scythe blade of fused vertebrae on a haft of long bone |
| Marrow Crush | a maul-head of knotted, lumpy bone bursts out of a femur |
| Spine Lash | a length of spine lengthens joint by joint as it swings |

- **His three trees (redone 2026-09-30 with the user, a combo of his ideas and ours; the data's source is `tools/skill_trees.py`): Ossuary · Carapace · the Count.**

- **Ossuary** (the dead, asked, not forced): Raise Skeleton · Bone Offering · Grave Tithe · Unearth · Ossuary Colossus · Death March · Bone Host · Reassemble · Bone Legion (level 30). Prayer Banner is gone.

- **Carapace** (bone spells and plate; the tree was "Bone"): Bone Lance · Bone Armor (Shield of Bones folded in: plates grow between him and his dead) · **Mantle** (the aura, the grit that hangs round him: his armour, his dead's shards and his spells' fuel; was Shard Aura) · Marrow Siphon · Charnel Cage (Bone Arms folded in: arms grip what the ribs close over) · Ossify · Bone Spurs · Shard Storm · Bone Rain (the Unfallen asked down) · **Pale Lord** (was Grave Spirit, which sought and broke the straight-bone rule: a warden of old bone on a white waymark that casts his bone spells again, dead straight) · Bone Mastery (level 30).

- **The Count** (melee and curses, his numerology): every melee blow cuts a notch into what it strikes, up to nine; nine closes the count. Melee: Bone Blade · Grinding Charge · Marrow Crush (reads the count: +11% a notch, then wipes it) · Scythe Sweep · Grave Leap · Spine Lash. Curses and the count: **Tally** (passive; the ninth notch lands at double force; blows come in threes: every third in a string cleaves one more) · **Open Count** (curse: anyone's hits notch the cursed; a closed count shatters it for a ninth of its life) · **The Fewer** (curse: the fewer of their kind near them, the more they take) · **The Weighing** (curse: each notch slows them and lightens their blows) · **The Ninth Stair** (level 24 curse on one enemy: nine blows take it down nine steps, each +9%; on the ninth, if low enough, it is finished; never bosses) · **Count Mastery** (level 30, Root of Nine: closed counts give back footing; every ninth kill crowns one of the standing dead). Carapace Mastery is gone.

- **The count, seen (the user, 2026-09-30):** a small white geometric sigil hangs ghostly over the head of whatever he curses: the curse's own figure in nine strands (Open Count the nine-pointed star; The Fewer nine spokes from one point; The Weighing a beam whose strands tip; The Ninth Stair nine steps going down). Each notch lights the next strand, so the lit strands are the count; no numeral (the user). At nine it swells and thins away. It breathes and turns slowly, never flickers, and the dark never hides it (`entities/count_sigil.gd`, test `--arena=4 --sigils`).

- **What is seen, skill by skill (the growth the lore won't name):** every skill is bone growing. **His own bone** grows straight and ordered; **bone in his enemies and the ground** grows wild: lumps, spurs, asymmetry. Raise Skeleton: the dead stand up out of his grit, straightening as they rise. Bone Offering: a skeleton bursts outward in a thicket of spurs. Grave Tithe: what was bone in the slain comes to him as grit. Unearth: the buried dead come up through the ground, spurred and fused. Ossuary Colossus: a hundred dead fused into one frame, seams and all. Death March: spurs rise in their footprints. Reassemble: broken dead come back together, a little lumpier each time. Bone Legion: more dead, larger than the men they were. Bone Lance: a spear grows out of the ground in a dead-straight line. Marrow Siphon: the struck wither and their bone shows through. Charnel Cage: ribs and arms grow up round the target and close. Ossify: its own bones overgrow and fuse; it shatters when struck. Bone Spurs: spurs erupt from whatever he has wounded. Shard Storm: the grit driven outward, every splinter straight. Bone Rain: shards of star-bone fall straight out of the dark. Pale Lord: a white stone, and a warden of old bone standing on it. Mantle: the ground spurs and splinters round him. Bone Armor: plates grow over him and between him and his dead. The Count: notches cut into bone you can see on the struck; a closing count cracks them through. Melee weapons grow from the relic bone (table above).

- **Art rule:** bone growth is drawn as material, never light: white-ochre bone creeping, knotting and cracking, with dust where it breaks.

- **Fortitude:** poise means more to him than to anyone; his notables reward staying on his feet.

## 8. The order's enemies

- **The Hush and its servants:** the enemy the Rule exists for. The Order hates the Silence the way a mason hates frost, because it takes with no rite at all.

- **The Order of the Gilded Peak** (the Empty Hand's order): the Peak gilded its dead and gave them to the Hush, and left no bone.

- To the Order that was the worst sacrilege in the world. So its standing dead went up the Peak's stair to carry the monks' bones home before the Hush could.

- What happened in the Peak's courtyard afterwards, the Order does not discuss (see `claude/godmarrow-class-kusho.md`).

- **The flesh-houses of the Red Mother** (the Hemomancer's brotherhoods): two mothers and one dead world between them. They have fought for centuries over the dead, the houses claiming the meat and the Order the frame.

- **The Hollow Mystic's lodges:** they keep souls and let bone lie. The Order calls it neglect, and the lodges call the standing dead a desecration.

- **The Shrine Keepers:** they give the dead to the water, and the Order's knights dredge the bones back out.

## 9. Look

**Direction:** the colossal stature of a golden praetorian guard, made entirely of **bone**, in no way gold. It is our own design, borrowing none of the original's heraldry.

**Silhouette:** the tallest hero in the game, a narrow tower, **a column with shoulders**. At isometric scale it reads as a vertical.
- **Crest:** a high one of stacked vertebrae.
- **Mantle:** long and hanging straight to the ground.

**Armour:** plate of carved and fitted bone, the relics of brothers who went before, each plate cut with a name.
- Old, ivory to ochre, crazed with fine cracks.
- **The unease:** one pauldron and the back of one gauntlet carry more bone than the rest. It's knotted and lumpy there, with a small spur curling out. Nobody carved that.

**Head:** a closed helm whose faceplate is a polished skull-cover, with only the eye sockets and teeth left dark.
- A ring of script runs round the brow. Not the Order's script. Nobody asks.

**Cloth:** a dust-grey mantle, heavy and weathered pale. It is embroidered in bone thread with the Order's sign: **a figure bowed under a bar across its shoulders.**

**Colour:** muted ivory-to-ochre bone and dust grey, with deep shadow in the joints.
- **The one bold mass:** a window of old **amber marrow-glass** in the breastplate, the Marrow orb's colour. It is a material, not a light: no glow.

**Around him:** hanging grit and splinters, and a faint trail of pale dust behind each step.

**Hands:** free. Weapons are separate layers, later.

**Motion:** unhurried and heavy. He never runs; he advances.
- **Idle:** a slight settle under load.
- **Cast:** both palms pressed upward, as if bracing a ceiling.
- **Dodge:** a grinding shoulder-roll.

## 10. Writing rules for him

- **Nouns:** bone, frame, marrow, well, chrism, niche, stair, ossuary, vigil, count, pogost, dust, grit, weight, chapter, warden, sweeper, relic.

- **Verbs:** bear, keep, lay, anoint, count, stand, sweep, weigh, finish, give back, ask.

- **The Russian register, used sparingly:**

- words: Kostyak, Pogost

- place names: plain and grim (the Ossuary of Nine Stairs, the Ninth Stair, the Sky Pogost)

- no real churches, saints or sacraments

- **Lovecraftian tells, always partial:**

- proportions slightly wrong

- a count that keeps rising

- a room nobody dug

- script that won't copy the same way twice

- weights that don't add up

- dust that never comes down

- skulls that turn to follow a lamp

- **Never:**

- say the Bone is growing (show it only in skills and art)

- explain the builders, the count or the weight

- pregnancy, mills or bellows, iron as a theme, or "the Knitting"

- eating dust

- anything Tibetan

- anything that glows
